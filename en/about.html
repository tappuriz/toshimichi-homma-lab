Research areas

Grounded in organizational behavior, I study organizational commitment, occupational commitment, extra-role behavior, and the management of professional work.

I am especially interested in how professionals such as pharmacists, nurses, and care workers reconcile commitment to their organization with commitment to their occupation, and how they build everyday work and relationships with others.

I am also expanding my research to include changes in work and roles brought by generative AI, and to connections between university education and external communities.

Graduate study

I supervise research students and master’s students in the Graduate School of Business Administration at Osaka University of Economics. International students are welcome. Current interests

Current interests

Organizational and occupational commitment among professionals

Professionals are members of organizations while also holding strong responsibility toward their work and occupation. I treat “how one relates to the organization” and “how one relates to the work” as distinct dimensions, and examine how differences and tensions appear—and how organizations can support people through them.

Generative AI adoption and organizations

I am examining developments in generative AI, especially how its use affects ways of working in teams, role division, decision-making, and learning at the individual and organizational levels.

Learning across university and external communities

I value learning that moves across classrooms, study groups, tech communities, and industry events— and bringing insights from outside the university back into education.

Running study groups and communities

Through Management & AI Commons Osaka, I explore how management scholarship can inform not only research and teaching but also placemaking and community practice—and whether universities still have room to connect people and knowledge and spark new activity.
